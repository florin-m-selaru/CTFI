Center for Therapeutics in Fibrosis and IBD

Bridging academic research and industry practice to develop therapeutics for fibrosis and IBD.

Inflammatory bowel disease has no cure, and several of its most serious complications have no approved drug therapy. Fibrotic strictures, which develop in up to half of patients with Crohn's disease, are one example.

From clinical need to therapy

Each program starts from a defined clinical problem. Research, development and validation are then repeated, and each iteration produces publications and the funding that supports the next. The intended end point is a therapy suitable for clinical testing.

Rationale

Research in IBD tends to follow the prevailing questions of the field, and approaches that fall outside them receive less attention. Engineers, for their part, have limited exposure to the clinical problems that gastroenterologists manage. The Center brings the two communities onto shared projects.

Fibrotic strictures are a local problem requiring a local solution.

Disciplines

Therapeutic development draws on expertise that is rarely held within a single laboratory. The Center organizes that expertise around individual clinical problems.

Clinical Medicine

Definition of the clinical need, disease biology, cell and animal models, and access to human tissue.

Science and Engineering

Medicinal chemistry, formulation science, and drug and device development. For many antifibrotic candidates, delivery rather than biology is the limiting step.

Translation

Intellectual property, contract research organizations, regulatory advice and industry partners.

Working with the Center

Investigators engage with the Center through informal advising, joint grant applications and collaborative projects. Clinicians seeking engineering or chemistry expertise, and engineers seeking a clinical application for a technology, are welcome to contact us. Projects remain with their investigators.
